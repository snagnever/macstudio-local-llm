Do uncensored weights cost speed?

Three uncensored Qwen3.8 Flash-Next packs against the standard pack on a Mac Studio M4 Max

Not measurably: 0% to 5% slower per agent turn, inside the noise. I measured three uncensored packs of Qwen3.8 Flash-Next against the standard pack, on the same server, at 32K, 128K and 256K of context. On average the uncensored packs write tokens a few percent slower. Everything else, from reading the prompt to reusing the cache, is the same. But two boots of one pack differ by up to 10%, which is more than the gap between the packs.

What I compared

One standard pack, three uncensored packs

An uncensored (or “abliterated”) pack is the same model with the refusal behaviour edited out of the weights. A “pack” here is one set of GGUF weight files. All four run on the same server, Splash 1.6.1, with the same settings.

The data rules out a large slowdown, and no more than that.

Limits

What this does not show

  • Two boots per pack and context. A difference under about 10% is inside the boot-to-boot spread.
  • Quantization is mixed in. Two of the three uncensored packs are IQ3_S; the standard pack is IQ4_XS. I did not run the standard pack at IQ3_S.
  • Heretic v2 is not the file as published. The server refused it; I recast 24 indexer tensors from F16 to BF16.
  • One machine, one server. Mac Studio M4 Max 128 GB and Splash 1.6.1. Another runtime may behave differently.
  • One stream. No concurrent requests.
  • Speed only. This page does not measure answer quality.

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: Swift 1.5 vs iQ-MLX

Two 4.7-bpw packs of the same model on the same runtime and flags: the ddalcu iQ-MLX pack (n2, the previous daily driver) and the Dankpaws Swift 1.5 fine-tune (w1). Six quality suites in two sampling profiles, time per question, tool-call latency and agent-turn speed, with the other October stacks as speed reference.

Swift 1.5 (w1) passes the three gates and is promoted to Flash-Next daily driver.

  • Same quality. Every suite stays inside its G1 margin in both sampling profiles. Per-suite differences are 0–3 questions on the gated grades, within one-run noise.
  • Time to the final answer. With the vendor sampling (t1), the median per question goes from 63.9 s to 22.3 s on GPQA (0.35×) and from 39.1 s to 19.1 s on LiveCodeBench (0.49×). Greedy (t0): 0.81× and 0.67×.
  • Same agent-turn speed. T_turn 7.9 s vs 8.1 s at 32K and 8.6 s vs 8.9 s at 128K, on the same runtime and prefix cache; decode 76.7 vs 73.2 tok/s at 128K.
  • Still open. Agent quality on Terminal-Bench is not measured. Rollback is one variable: FLASHNEXT_MODEL=<iQ pack>.

Not measured: agent quality. Terminal-Bench 2.0 needs a second machine for the Docker containers, so this campaign suspended it. The driver script is ready (bench/terminal-bench/scripts/run-tbench-flashnext.sh). Speed by context up to 512K for the other runtimes is on the perf-lines page.

Limits

  • One run per arm and profile. GPQA and LiveCodeBench use 50-question samples (seed 42), not the full sets.
  • The IF probe is an IFEval-style check with 20 cases. It does not reproduce the IFBench score of the Swift 1.5 card.
  • Tool calls (jdhodges, Veerman): the runner sent reasoning_effort and the sampling, but did not record the reasoning text in this campaign (n/d in the tables). A median of 104–194 tokens per answer (by arm, suite and profile) suggests little or no thinking in these two suites.
  • The two packs have the same layout, but their n-gram tables differ (different LFS hashes): the A/B compares the packs, not only the fine-tune.
  • t1 and t0 of each arm ran in different server starts; the battery paused twice for 512K runs of another campaign (at profile boundaries, no data lost).
  • Speed rows other than n2 and w1 come from earlier sessions: o1, m1 and d1 from the October campaign, c1 from the September campaign (mlx-serve 26.9.2). In the October campaign n2 measured within about 2% of its value here, which bounds the drift between those sessions; it says nothing about the September session.
  • Agent quality (Terminal-Bench 2.0) is not measured for either arm.
